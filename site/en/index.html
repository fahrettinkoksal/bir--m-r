One life, end to end.

You don't get to pick the family you're born into. After that it's one decision a year: school, work, money, love, health, sometimes a courtroom. Seventy years later, what's left is the sum of what you chose.

The three lives below start from the same baby: the same parents, the same city, the same year. One ends at 56, another runs to 86. The only thing that changed is the decisions — the game wrote the rest.

Same seed, same birth — one ends at 56 after a long illness, one leaves four children and nine grandchildren at 78, one runs to 86. Not one line was written for this site; the three full logs come to 2,764 lines, and at most three per year made it here. The game is in Turkish; these lines are translated from its own output.

Bir Ömür ("a lifetime") is a deep life simulation: school, work, family, money, health, law, hobbies, generations. All of it inside one life, and all of it reading the rest. The track you pick at school decides which jobs you can take; your health decides whether you can keep one; a court decision decides whether your salary arrives. What you choose at twenty finds you at forty.

Its world isn't built from invented numbers: the economy, wages and prices are anchored — by measurement — to Turkey today. The events, texts and rules were written from scratch for this game; not one line was carried over from another. The game is in Turkish; this page is the English summary.

The game isn't in any store yet; it is shared with communities as an open beta. Three platforms are the target: PC, mobile and Apple. Every number below was measured from today's repository.

What's in it

One year, one "age up" button. Behind it, systems that talk to each other: a choice in one of them finds you in another, years later.

School and work

Ten school stages from primary to university, 20 university programmes, 13 school clubs. 55 professions, with interviews, raises, promotions, mastery and retirement.

Family

Parents, siblings, step-family. Marriage, a family plan, pregnancy, grandchildren. You sit down with your child's homework, hand out pocket money, set rules — and when a generation ends, the game can continue as your child.

Money

Cost of living, two banks and loans, a property and vehicle market across 22 cities, rental income with real tenants, an investment portfolio and a business of your own from 14 types.

Health

Eleven offences in eight categories, three tiers of lawyer. Investigation, bail, trial, prison and probation. In detention no salary arrives and seniority freezes.

Becoming a parent

Your own first eighteen years are full: school stages, a teacher's name, classmates, pocket money, clubs. We wanted the same once you become a parent. A child is not a notification line; they are someone with a record of their own, growing up.

  • A baby on the way — A pending birth shows in three places at once: the character header, the top of the life log and the Relationships screen. The baby arrives at the next age-up. The game never promises what it cannot keep: if the other parent is no longer alive, it does not say "will be born".
  • Family plan — You talk it over with your spouse or partner and decide: we are trying, not for now, or we haven't discussed it. The decision is recorded and brought back when you are alone together. There is no "have a child" button — a child is a possibility, not a guarantee.
  • Things only a parent can do — Sit down with the homework, give pocket money, sign them up for a hobby, set a rule. Each one changes the child's own record: intelligence, savings, interests. Pocket money leaves your wallet and lands in theirs; at eighteen, that balance decides where they start.
  • Age stages — Baby, child, teenager, adult. What you can do changes with the stage: a baby's card never shows homework or rules, and you do not set rules for a grown child. A teenager can refuse.
  • The child's year — When the year ends, the child gets a summary too: what happened, and what your effort changed. The numbers come from the difference between the record at the start of the year and today — measured, not guessed.

Rules are not popular: the child's mood drops and some distance creeps in. What you get back is a lower chance of trouble at school for a while — not a guarantee, and it does nothing at all with a child you have grown apart from. All of this was measured across 500 family lifetimes; the rates come out of the game's own records rather than being written by hand.

Under the surface

A few buttons on screen. Underneath, engines that run in order every year. Everything below is in the game and working today.

  • Market regime — Assets don't roll dice separately. The year has a regime — flat, normal, strong, crisis — and two hidden parameters: inflation pressure and market confidence. Those produce risk appetite and demand for safety; shares and funds answer to the first, gold and currency to the second, each with its own weight. In a crisis year gold can hold a portfolio while shares fall — but no two crises play out the same.
  • Fictional companies — Twelve companies across ten sectors carry hidden indicators that move year to year. A good company can stay good for years, a bad one can recover; management quality changes slowly and softens bad news. Company news isn't a random headline — it comes out of that state.
  • A business of your own — 14 business types in three scales. Price, staff, upkeep, advertising and reputation are each managed separately; demand is computed from price elasticity against the market average. 102 business events: inspections, disasters, staff trouble, an ad that lands.
  • Property and rent — Rent comes from the city coefficient and the flat's condition, not from a catalogue price. The tenant has an occupation, a payment history and a contract; deposit, vacancy, move-out and a maintenance ledger are all kept. A flat in Istanbul and one in Amasya do not earn the same rent.

  • Banks and debt — Two banks, different rates and different approval strictness. An application is genuinely assessed; the answer is not a dice roll. Miss an instalment and the debt grows with interest — the wallet never goes negative, the debt accumulates.
  • Law — Eleven offence types in eight categories, three tiers of lawyer. Investigation, bail, trial, prison and probation each run separately. In detention no salary arrives and seniority freezes — but the job doesn't end, it waits for you.
  • Health — Six chronic conditions, eight health crises, a check-up report across six body systems. Each of the five stats has its own ageing curve; sport, the barber, books and courses measurably slow the decline. At critical health, ageing up locks: you have to decide first.
  • Family and generations — Step- and half-siblings, in-laws, grandchildren, bloodlines. Pregnancy is a process: a protection choice, an age-dependent chance, twins, fertility treatment. A divorce splits what was bought during the marriage; what came before it, plus inheritance and gifts, stays personal property. Custody, alimony, elder care and inheritance each have their own rules. When a generation ends, the game continues as your child.
  • The early years — 31 events for infancy and the years before school: household decisions, the neighbourhood, the first errand to the corner shop, street games, the first day of nursery, holiday pocket money, sibling jealousy. Four of them come back years later — the child who had the training wheels taken off rides at the front, the one who saved breaks the piggy bank, the one who learned letters early reads aloud in class.
  • The threshold years — 29 events for ages 16 to 20: exam prep, the preference list, results night, graduation, apprenticeship, a driving licence, the first job application, sharing a first salary with the household, moving out, a friend group scattering. Four come back years later; the school group ends up around one table again.
  • The home you live in — moving into your own place no longer makes life go quiet: the boiler dies mid-winter, the roof leaks, mould creeps into the bathroom, the building votes on a fee rise, the upstairs flat drags furniture at midnight, the flat next door goes up for sale. 28 events; seven hand your own earlier choice back years later — the patched boiler returns, the postponed roof costs twice as much, the insurance you bought pays off.
  • Neighbours — the building is no longer just text, it is a door: move into your own place or a rental and you get to know one to three neighbours. They have names, they age, you carry their shopping bags, a covered pot arrives at your door, you talk about the midnight noise, you end up on the same side at the building meeting. 12 events; four hand your own earlier choice back years later. Moving ends the neighbourhood, but if you were close they stay as friends.
  • Furnishing your home — a home is no longer an empty box: a fridge, washing machine, oven, bedroom set, sofa and dining table make it liveable; a television, air conditioner, dishwasher, rug, curtains and bookcase add the comfort. 18 items in a new "Home and living" store. The "state of your home" screen shows at a glance what is done and what is missing; furniture wears down over the years and needs servicing. When you move, your furniture comes with you.
  • Your own look — dark mode is now your call: follow the device, stay light, or keep it dark. The choice is stored with your save.
  • Trim it to taste — Features added later can each be switched off in settings. A module you turn off never appears in the game, the rest keeps working exactly as before, and the same seed still plays out the same way. Each row says what you lose by turning it off.
  • Career — 55 professions, a pool of 167 interview questions. Mastery and reputation build up over years; a hobby you kept for years earns a second chance at interview and a head start in mastery. The first two days of sick leave are unpaid, and long leave makes an employer uneasy.
  • No back doors — The market advances once per age and the index is written to the save: you cannot re-roll a price by closing the screen, by trading, or by loading an older save. Repeating the same action within one year to farm rewards is closed too, and a person who turned down an invitation stays turned down for that year.

The ordinary life

A life isn't only school, work and money. The book you read, the instrument you played for years, the cat you kept, the city you moved to — those are in the game too. None of them is a one-off button: they accrue over years and pay off later.

Hobbies

12 lasting hobbies — music, painting, reading, sport, cooking, photography, dance, chess, writing, gardening, languages, software. Each has five stages: Keen, Curious, Regular, Devoted, Master.

Reading

22 books in five genres, read page by page. Titles and authors are written for this game; no copyrighted text is used. Finishing a book raises intelligence, and a regular reader's mind erodes more slowly with age.

Sport

Running, weights, stretching. Six martial arts across 58 ranks, with their own tournament circuits. Plus a professional football career from the school team to retirement — never played at school, and that door stays shut.

Courses and clubs

39 actions across nine venues: courses, the library, the gym, the barber, a health centre, cosmetic clinics, entertainment. 13 school clubs; the first lessons are free, later ones are paid.

Pets

Ten species. Lifespans follow the real animal; illness, escape and the vet are all in. When a pet dies, the life diary says so.

Travel and cities

Six tour packages, plus moving house. Each of 22 cities has its own character: prices, work, schooling and health differ. You can only move to a nearby province.

Social media and fame

Posts, followers, sponsorships. Past a fame threshold, 14 media opportunities open; ignore it and fame decays. Every brand is fictional.

Life goals

21 goals in five areas: education, career, money, family, yourself. Tracked year by year on their own screen.

What we won't do

Life simulation is a genre that gets cheap easily. A few limits were set at the start.

No copied content

Events, text and art are written for this game. Nothing is carried over from another title.

No real names

Companies, banks, vehicle models and celebrities are fictional. No real person or brand is used.

No how-to

The crime and law system describes consequences. No text describes a usable method.

Not financial advice

The in-game market is fiction and isn't presented as a real recommendation.

Status

Where things actually stand today.

  • Tech — Flutter, one codebase.
  • Platforms — PC, mobile and Apple; we are aiming at all three. Today an Android and a Windows package are built on every push; the Apple build isn't there yet.
  • Playable — a life runs end to end: birth, school, work, family, ageing, death and the next generation.
  • Release — not in any store. The open beta is shared with communities.
  • Robustness — 3,414 automated tests run on every push; when a bug turns up, the test that catches it is written first.
  • Next — interface rough edges, content depth and balance decisions. No dates: the beta comes first.
