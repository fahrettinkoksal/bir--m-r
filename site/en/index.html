One life, end to end.

You don't get to pick the family you're born into. After that it's one decision a year: school, work, money, love, health, sometimes a courtroom. Seventy years later, what's left is the sum of what you chose.

Bir Ömür ("a lifetime") is a mobile life simulation set in Turkey. The events, rules and economy are written from scratch for this game, and the numbers — from the minimum wage to apartment prices — are anchored to Turkey in 2026. The game is in Turkish; this page is the English summary.

What's in it

One year, one "age up" button. Behind it, systems that talk to each other: a choice in one of them finds you in another, years later.

School and work

Primary school through university, exams and tracks. 55 professions, with interviews, raises, promotions, mastery and retirement.

Family

Parents, siblings, step-family. Marriage, children, grandchildren — and when a generation ends, the game can continue as your child.

Money

Cost of living, banks and loans, buying a home or a car, rental income, an investment portfolio and your own business.

Health

Stats that decline with age, chronic illness, critical health crises — and upkeep that measurably pays off.

Law

Investigation, bail, a lawyer, trial, prison and probation. In pre-trial detention no salary arrives and seniority freezes.

Life

Hobbies, sport, social media, pets, travel, life goals and more than 400 events.

How it's built

Bir Ömür is written by one person and one AI: the design decisions are Fahrettin Köksal's, most of the code was written with Claude Code. The interesting part isn't the tool — it's the discipline around it.

  • Every rule is written down. The game's 180 settled decisions live numbered in one file, and the code that implements one cites its number. A test makes every number cited in code mandatory to exist.
  • An unapproved number is never "final". Every balance value stays tagged prototypeOnly until it's decided. If a constant stops being read and goes dead, a separate test breaks.
  • Measure, don't guess. A bot plays the game birth-to-death thousands of times, and balance calls are made from those runs. No value gets changed because it "feels better".
  • The screens get read. Tests play the game through its own interface and dump every piece of text on screen. That dump has read seven hard-to-reach states, from prison to pregnancy, and found eight real bugs.
  • No test is deleted or loosened. When a fix breaks an old expectation, the expectation is rewritten or the bug is accepted — never skipped to get back to green.

What we won't do

Life simulation is a genre that gets cheap easily. A few limits were set at the start.

No copied content

Events, text and art are written for this game. Nothing is carried over from another title.

No real names

Companies, banks, vehicle models and celebrities are fictional. No real person or brand is used.

No how-to

The crime and law system describes consequences. No text describes a usable method.

Not financial advice

The in-game market is fiction and isn't presented as a real recommendation.

Status

Where things actually stand today.

  • Playable — a life runs end to end: birth, school, work, family, ageing, death and the next generation.
  • Release — not in any store, no open beta. Every push builds an Android and a Windows package automatically; those are for closed testing.
  • Next — interface rough edges, content depth and balance decisions. No dates: we'd rather test first.
