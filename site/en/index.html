One life, end to end.

You don't get to pick the family you're born into. After that it's one decision a year: school, work, money, love, health, sometimes a courtroom. Seventy years later, what's left is the sum of what you chose.

Bir Ömür ("a lifetime") is a mobile life simulation set in Turkey. The events, rules and economy are written from scratch for this game, and the numbers — from the minimum wage to apartment prices — are anchored to Turkey in 2026. The game is in Turkish; this page is the English summary.

It is not a menu of buttons. The year's market regime, the health of the companies, a tenant's payment history, what detention does to your salary — all of it runs inside the same year, each part reading the others.

What's in it

One year, one "age up" button. Behind it, systems that talk to each other: a choice in one of them finds you in another, years later.

These are the app's own screens, captured from the real interface — not photographs of a phone.

School and work

Ten school stages from primary to university, 20 university programmes, 13 school clubs. 55 professions, with interviews, raises, promotions, mastery and retirement.

Family

Parents, siblings, step-family. Marriage, children, grandchildren — and when a generation ends, the game can continue as your child.

Money

Cost of living, two banks and loans, a property and vehicle market across 22 cities, rental income with real tenants, an investment portfolio and a business of your own from 14 types.

Health

Five stats that decline with age, six chronic conditions, eight crises, check-ups and cosmetic work. Upkeep measurably pays off.

Law

Eleven offences in eight categories, three tiers of lawyer. Investigation, bail, trial, prison and probation. In detention no salary arrives and seniority freezes.

Life

Hobbies, sport, books, social media, pets, travel, life goals and 461 events — all of it below.

Under the surface

A few buttons on screen. Underneath, engines that run in order every year. Everything below is in the game and working today.

  • Market regime — Assets don't roll dice separately. The year has a regime — flat, normal, strong, crisis — and two hidden parameters: inflation pressure and market confidence. Those produce risk appetite and demand for safety; shares and funds answer to the first, gold and currency to the second, each with its own weight. In a crisis year gold can hold a portfolio while shares fall — but no two crises play out the same.
  • Fictional companies — Twelve companies across ten sectors carry hidden indicators that move year to year. A good company can stay good for years, a bad one can recover; management quality changes slowly and softens bad news. Company news isn't a random headline — it comes out of that state.
  • A business of your own — 14 business types in three scales. Price, staff, upkeep, advertising and reputation are each managed separately; demand is computed from price elasticity against the market average. 102 business events: inspections, disasters, staff trouble, an ad that lands.
  • Property and rent — Rent comes from the city coefficient and the flat's condition, not from a catalogue price. The tenant has an occupation, a payment history and a contract; deposit, vacancy, move-out and a maintenance ledger are all kept. A flat in Istanbul and one in Amasya do not earn the same rent.
  • Banks and debt — Two banks, different rates and different approval strictness. An application is genuinely assessed; the answer is not a dice roll. Miss an instalment and the debt grows with interest — the wallet never goes negative, the debt accumulates.
  • Law — Eleven offence types in eight categories, three tiers of lawyer. Investigation, bail, trial, prison and probation each run separately. In detention no salary arrives and seniority freezes — but the job doesn't end, it waits for you.
  • Health — Six chronic conditions, eight health crises, a check-up report across six body systems. Each of the five stats has its own ageing curve; sport, the barber, books and courses measurably slow the decline. At critical health, ageing up locks: you have to decide first.
  • Family and generations — Step- and half-siblings, in-laws, grandchildren, bloodlines. A divorce splits what was bought during the marriage; what came before it, plus inheritance and gifts, stays personal property. Custody, alimony, elder care and inheritance each have their own rules. When a generation ends, the game continues as your child.
  • Career — 55 professions, a pool of 167 interview questions. Mastery and reputation build up over years; a hobby you kept for years earns a second chance at interview and a head start in mastery. The first two days of sick leave are unpaid, and long leave makes an employer uneasy.
  • No back doors — The market advances once per age and the index is written to the save: you cannot re-roll a price by closing the screen, by trading, or by loading an older save. Repeating the same action within one year to farm rewards is closed too, and a person who turned down an invitation stays turned down for that year.

The ordinary life

A life isn't only school, work and money. The book you read, the instrument you played for years, the cat you kept, the city you moved to — those are in the game too. None of them is a one-off button: they accrue over years and pay off later.

Hobbies

12 lasting hobbies — music, painting, reading, sport, cooking, photography, dance, chess, writing, gardening, languages, software. Each has five stages: Keen, Curious, Regular, Devoted, Master.

Reading

22 books in five genres, read page by page. Titles and authors are written for this game; no copyrighted text is used. Finishing a book raises intelligence, and a regular reader's mind erodes more slowly with age.

Sport

Running, weights, stretching. Six martial arts across 58 ranks, with their own tournament circuits. Plus a professional football career from the school team to retirement — never played at school, and that door stays shut.

Courses and clubs

39 actions across nine venues: courses, the library, the gym, the barber, a health centre, cosmetic clinics, entertainment. 13 school clubs; the first lessons are free, later ones are paid.

Pets

Ten species. Lifespans follow the real animal; illness, escape and the vet are all in. When a pet dies, the life diary says so.

Travel and cities

Six tour packages, plus moving house. Each of 22 cities has its own character: prices, work, schooling and health differ. You can only move to a nearby province.

Social media and fame

Posts, followers, sponsorships. Past a fame threshold, 14 media opportunities open; ignore it and fame decays. Every brand is fictional.

Life goals

21 goals in five areas: education, career, money, family, yourself. Tracked year by year on their own screen.

What we won't do

Life simulation is a genre that gets cheap easily. A few limits were set at the start.

No copied content

Events, text and art are written for this game. Nothing is carried over from another title.

No real names

Companies, banks, vehicle models and celebrities are fictional. No real person or brand is used.

No how-to

The crime and law system describes consequences. No text describes a usable method.

Not financial advice

The in-game market is fiction and isn't presented as a real recommendation.

Status

Where things actually stand today.

  • Tech — Flutter; Android first, with a Windows build as well.
  • Playable — a life runs end to end: birth, school, work, family, ageing, death and the next generation.
  • Release — not in any store. The open beta is shared with communities; every push builds an Android and a Windows package automatically.
  • Robustness — 3,414 automated tests run on every push; when a bug turns up, the test that catches it is written first.
  • Next — interface rough edges, content depth and balance decisions. No dates: the beta comes first.
